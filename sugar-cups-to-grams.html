Sugar cups to grams

White, caster, icing, raw and demerara sugar all weigh different amounts by the cup, and they go by different names in the US, UK and Australia. Here is what each one weighs and what to buy.

Updated 12 November 2026 · Sources listed at the end

Advertisement

Quick answer: one cup of white (granulated) sugar weighs about 200 g (USDA; King Arthur Baking says 198 g). Caster sugar is about the same. Icing sugar is about 120 g unsifted and 100 g sifted. Raw (turbinado) sugar is about 180 g and demerara about 220 g.

The converter above starts on white sugar. Change the ingredient for caster, icing, raw, demerara or brown sugar, or switch the units to Australian cups and tablespoons.

Why the weights differ

Granulated sugar is the easy one. Its crystals flow like dry sand and fill a cup the same way every time, which is why its cup weight barely changes between American sources. Icing sugar is the opposite: it is ground to a powder, it traps air like flour does, and it compacts in the bag. Sifting fluffs it up again, which is why the USDA lists an unsifted cup at 120 g and a sifted cup at 100 g. When a recipe says "1 cup icing sugar, sifted", measure first and sift afterwards; "1 cup sifted icing sugar" means sift first, then measure the lighter result.

Raw and demerara sugars have large, coarse crystals that leave more air between them, though how a cup actually fills depends on the brand's crystal size. King Arthur Baking weighs a cup of turbinado at 180 g and demerara at 220 g. Caster sugar sits right beside granulated: King Arthur weighs its superfine sugar slightly lighter, at 190 g, while Australian charts give caster and white sugar the same weight. For a recipe, treat them as interchangeable by weight.

Australian charts weigh sugar heavier

Australian conversion charts tend to give bigger numbers. The Australian Women's Weekly and New Idea both list white sugar at 220 g per 250 mL cup, and New Idea gives caster sugar the same. Scaling the US figure up to a 250 mL cup gives about 208 g, so the Australian charts are roughly 5% heavier. Their icing sugar figures are further apart again: 160 g (Women's Weekly) and 155 g (New Idea) per cup, against about 125 g scaled from the USDA. Neither chart explains how its cups were filled; the likeliest reason is a firmer, scooped cup. If an Australian recipe gives grams, use them; if it gives only cups, its author may well have measured that firmer way.

Icing sugar is where the names hide a real difference. American powdered sugar has a little cornstarch mixed in to stop it clumping, as King Arthur Baking notes. In Australia you choose: CSR's icing sugar mixture lists cane sugar with tapioca or maize starch, while pure icing sugar is sugar alone. British icing sugar such as Silver Spoon's uses a small amount of anti-caking agent rather than starch. For dusting and buttercream the difference rarely shows. When a recipe asks specifically for pure icing sugar, buy pure.

Out of caster sugar?

Make your own. King Arthur Baking's advice is to blitz granulated sugar in a blender or food processor until it is fine, then measure it by weight. Weigh rather than use cups, because the finer sugar fills a cup differently. Let the dust settle before you lift the lid.
