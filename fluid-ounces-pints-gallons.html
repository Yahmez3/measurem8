Fluid ounces, pints, quarts and gallons: US vs UK

Americans and Britons use the same names for different amounts. A British pint holds a fifth more than an American one, and the fluid ounce goes the other way. Here are the exact sizes and the conversions you actually need.

Updated 12 November 2026 · Sources listed at the end

Advertisement

Quick answer: a US fluid ounce is 29.57 mL and a UK fluid ounce is 28.41 mL. A US pint is 473 mL (16 fl oz) and a UK pint is 568 mL (20 fl oz). A US gallon is 3.785 L and a UK gallon is 4.546 L. The UK pint, quart and gallon are all about 20% bigger than the American ones.

The converter above starts at one US fluid ounce. The unit lists include both the US and the UK versions of the fluid ounce, pint, quart and gallon, so pick the one your recipe or label means.

Two things cause the confusion. The gallons were defined differently from the start: the US kept an old British wine gallon of 231 cubic inches, while Britain replaced its gallons in 1824 with a new imperial gallon, now fixed at exactly 4.54609 litres. And the pints are divided differently: a US pint has 16 fluid ounces, a UK pint has 20. The result is that the American fluid ounce is slightly bigger, but every American pint, quart and gallon is smaller.

How the units fit together

In the US, 1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 16 cups = 128 fluid ounces. In the UK, 1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 160 fluid ounces. The pint-to-gallon steps match; only the number of fluid ounces in a pint differs.

Fluid ounces are not ounces

A fluid ounce measures volume. An ounce measures weight: 28.35 g. The names are similar because the 1824 imperial gallon was defined by the weight of water it held, which set the imperial fluid ounce at almost exactly the space taken up by one ounce of water. So for water the two are nearly the same: a UK fluid ounce of water weighs 1.002 oz, and a US fluid ounce of water weighs 1.04 oz.

For anything other than water the two part company quickly. A US fluid ounce of honey weighs about 42 g, nearly one and a half ounces. So when a recipe lists "8 oz honey", it means weight, not a cup; when it says "8 fl oz milk", it means a cup's worth of volume. The old rhymes capture the same idea: in America, "a pint's a pound the world around" (a US pint of water weighs 1.04 lb), while British cooks learned that "a pint of water weighs a pound and a quarter" (a UK pint of water weighs 1.25 lb). Both are right for their own pint.

Gallons and fuel economy

Because the UK gallon is 20% bigger, the same car travels 20% further on a UK gallon, so British miles-per-gallon figures are always higher than American ones for the same car. To convert, multiply US mpg by 1.201 to get UK mpg. Much of the world uses litres per 100 km instead, where lower is better: divide 235.2 by US mpg, or 282.5 by UK mpg.

Sources

  • US units: NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C: the US gallon is 231 cubic inches (3.785411784 L); 1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 128 fluid ounces, so 1 fluid ounce = 1/128 gallon = 29.5735295625 mL.
  • UK units: Weights and Measures Act 1985, Schedule 1: gallon 4.54609 cubic decimetres; quart one-quarter gallon; pint one-eighth gallon; fluid ounce one-twentieth of a pint (one-160th of a gallon).
  • Weight comparisons use the international avoirdupois ounce (28.349523125 g) and pound (453.59237 g), the same definitions used by the converter, and water at about 1 g per mL. Honey uses MeasureMate's figure of 340 g per 240 mL cup.
  • Fuel economy: L/100 km = 235.215 / US mpg = 282.481 / UK mpg, from the gallon and mile definitions above (1 mile = 1.609344 km).
