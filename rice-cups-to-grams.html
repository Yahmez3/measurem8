Rice cups to grams, uncooked and cooked

A cup of uncooked rice and a cup of cooked rice are very different amounts. Here is what each weighs, how much rice to cook per person, and how much water to use.

Updated 12 November 2026 · Sources listed at the end

Advertisement

Quick answer: one cup of uncooked white long-grain rice weighs about 185 g (USDA). One cup of cooked white rice weighs about 158 g. A cup of uncooked rice makes about 3 cups cooked, so allow roughly 60 g (1/3 cup) uncooked per person for a cup of cooked rice each.

The converter above uses uncooked white rice. Change the ingredient to brown rice, or to cooked white or brown rice, and switch to Australian cups if you need them.

Sources vary a little for uncooked rice. The USDA gives 185 g a cup for long-grain white rice, while King Arthur Baking weighs half a cup of dry long-grain rice at 99 g, which works out to about 198 g a cup. Brands and grain types vary a little, but for everyday cooking the difference will not show.

Cooked rice: cups to grams

Cooked rice1/2 cup1 cup1 AU cup (250 mL)
White long-grain79 g158 g165 g
Brown85 g170 g177 g

Cooked rice weighs less by the cup than uncooked rice: each grain swells with water, and the soft grains sit loosely in the cup. Put the two together and the change is striking: a cup of uncooked white rice (185 g) becomes about three cups of cooked rice, around 475 g, roughly two and a half times its starting weight. Cooked weights depend on how firmly you pack the cup, so spoon cooked rice in loosely, the way it falls from the pot.

How much rice per person

The USA Rice Federation's rule of thumb is that one cup of dry rice makes three cups cooked. A cup of cooked rice is a generous side or a modest main, and the table below uses that. For a lighter side, halve it: the Australian Dietary Guidelines count half a cup of cooked rice as one serve of grains.

How much water to cook rice

The absorption method, where the rice soaks up all the water in a covered pot, depends on the right ratio. These come from rice producers' own cooking guides. Always check the packet too, because varieties and brands differ.

The ratio works with any cup, as long as you use the same cup for the rice and the water. That makes rice the one kitchen job where it does not matter whether your cup is American or Australian.

Why rice charts disagree

Australian charts can be confusing for rice. The Australian Women's Weekly lists rice at 200 g per 250 mL cup, and New Idea lists it at 155 g, and neither says whether it means uncooked or cooked. The 200 g figure is in line with uncooked rice; 155 g is close to a cup of cooked rice, so the two charts may not be measuring the same thing. Check whether your recipe means cooked or uncooked rice before converting. The difference is threefold.
